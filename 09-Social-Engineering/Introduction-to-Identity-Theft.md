
# Introduction to Identity Theft

## Overview

**Identity theft** occurs when someone steals another person's personal or identifying information with the intention of deceiving others or committing fraud.

Identity theft is a crime and can affect individuals as well as organizations.

Attackers may use stolen identities to:
- Access personal or organizational accounts.
- Commit financial fraud.
- Obtain unauthorized services.
- Impersonate employees or other trusted individuals.
- Gain physical access to an organization.

## What Information Can Be Stolen?

Identity thieves may target different types of personal information, including:

- Full name and date of birth.
- National identification numbers.
- Phone numbers and email addresses.
- Home addresses.
- Bank account and payment card details.
- Usernames and passwords.
- Copies of identity documents.

## Identity Theft vs. Identity Fraud

These terms are closely related but have slightly different meanings.

- **Identity Theft:** The unauthorized acquisition of another person's identifying information.
- **Identity Fraud:** The use of stolen identity information to deceive others or obtain something unlawfully.

For example, stealing someone's payment card information is identity theft. Using that information to make unauthorized purchases is identity fraud.

## Potential Consequences

Identity theft can result in:

- Financial losses.
- Unauthorized transactions.
- Loss of access to personal accounts.
- Damage to personal or professional reputation.
- Legal and administrative problems.
- Unauthorized access to organizational resources.

## Summary

Identity theft involves stealing personal information to impersonate another person. Attackers may use stolen identities for financial fraud, unauthorized account access, or even physical access to organizations.
