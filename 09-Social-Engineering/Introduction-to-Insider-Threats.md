
# Introduction to Insider Threats

## Overview

An **insider threat** is a security risk that originates from someone who has authorized access to an organization's systems, networks, facilities, or sensitive information.

An insider may be an employee or another trusted individual with access to privileged organizational assets.

Unlike external attackers, insiders may already have legitimate access to the resources they target. This can make their activities difficult to detect and prevent.

## Why Are Insider Attacks Dangerous?

According to the course material, insider attacks have several characteristics:

- They can be relatively easy to launch.
- Prevention can be difficult.
- Insiders may have access to sensitive organizational resources.
- An insider may be able to succeed without bypassing external security controls.
- Identifying the person responsible can be challenging.

## Types of Insider Threats

### 1. Malicious Insider

A malicious insider intentionally steals, damages, or exposes organizational data.

For example, a dissatisfied employee may steal confidential files or deploy malware to damage company systems.

### 2. Negligent Insider

A negligent insider causes security problems through carelessness or a lack of security awareness.

Examples include:
- Ignoring security procedures.
- Sharing confidential information accidentally.
- Bypassing security controls.
- Leaving sensitive information exposed.

### 3. Professional Insider

A professional insider uses technical knowledge to identify an organization's weaknesses and vulnerabilities, then provides or sells that information to competitors or on black markets.

### 4. Compromised Insider

A compromised insider refers to an external attacker who gains access to an organization's internal resources or systems, potentially by compromising a legitimate employee's account.

This may allow the attacker to operate using trusted access.

## Summary

Insider threats originate from trusted access or compromised internal accounts. They can be malicious, negligent, professional, or the result of an external attacker gaining internal access.

Understanding these categories helps organizations identify and manage internal security risks.
