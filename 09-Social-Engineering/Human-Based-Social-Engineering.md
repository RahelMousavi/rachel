
# Human-Based Social Engineering

Human-Based Social Engineering involves exploiting human behavior, trust, and psychological weaknesses to obtain sensitive information or gain unauthorized access.

Attackers use direct interaction with individuals rather than relying entirely on technical methods.

## 1. Impersonation

Impersonation occurs when an attacker pretends to be a legitimate or authorized person to deceive a target.

Common impersonation scenarios include:

- **Regular Employee:** The attacker pretends to be an internal employee and requests sensitive information.
- **Important Person:** The attacker impersonates a manager, VIP, or important customer to create pressure.
- **Technical Support:** The attacker pretends to be an IT technician or support specialist and requests login credentials or other information.

## 2. Eavesdropping

Eavesdropping is the unauthorized listening to or reading of private conversations and messages without the knowledge of the people involved.

Attackers may use this technique to collect confidential information shared during conversations.

## 3. Shoulder Surfing

Shoulder Surfing is a technique in which an attacker obtains sensitive information by directly observing another person's screen or keyboard.

Examples include:

- Watching someone enter a password.
- Observing PIN entry at an ATM.
- Looking at confidential information displayed on a screen.

This technique can occur in public places such as airports, internet cafés, and public transportation.

Attackers may also use cameras or binoculars to observe targets from a distance.

## 4. Dumpster Diving

Dumpster Diving involves searching through discarded materials to find sensitive or valuable information.

Potentially exposed information includes:

- Contact information.
- Financial documents.
- Organizational or operational information.
- Passwords written on sticky notes.

Attackers may search company trash bins, printers, desks, or areas surrounding buildings.

## 5. Piggybacking

Piggybacking occurs when an authorized person intentionally or unintentionally allows an unauthorized individual to enter a restricted area with them.

For example, an attacker may claim to have forgotten their access card and ask an employee to let them in.

## 6. Tailgating

Tailgating occurs when an unauthorized person enters a restricted area by following an authorized individual through a secured entrance without their knowledge.

### Piggybacking vs. Tailgating

- **Piggybacking:** The authorized person knowingly or unknowingly allows the unauthorized person to enter.
- **Tailgating:** The unauthorized person follows the authorized person without their knowledge.

## Summary

Human-Based Social Engineering relies on direct interaction and the exploitation of human behavior.

Common techniques include impersonation, eavesdropping, shoulder surfing, dumpster diving, piggybacking, and tailgating.
