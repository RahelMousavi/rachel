
# Stages of Social Engineering Attacks

A social engineering attack generally involves three main stages.

## 1. Target Selection

In this stage, the attacker identifies a suitable target.

The target may be:

- An individual employee.
- A specific department.
- An organization's technical support team.
- An organization as a whole.

The attacker selects a target based on the information or access they want to obtain.

## 2. Building Communication

After selecting the target, the attacker establishes communication.

The attacker may attempt to:

- Build trust with the target.
- Establish a believable identity.
- Create a convincing situation.
- Gain the target's confidence.

The objective is to make the target more likely to respond to requests or disclose information.

## 3. Attack

In the final stage, the attacker uses the established communication or trust to achieve the intended objective.

This may involve:

- Obtaining confidential information.
- Collecting login credentials.
- Gaining unauthorized access.
- Convincing the victim to perform a specific action.

## Summary

A social engineering attack can be divided into three stages: target selection, building communication, and attack.

Understanding these stages helps security professionals recognize suspicious interactions and identify potential attacks before sensitive information is exposed.
