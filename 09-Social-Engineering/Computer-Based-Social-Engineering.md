
# Computer-Based Social Engineering

Computer-Based Social Engineering uses computers, websites, emails, pop-up windows, and online communication platforms to manipulate users into revealing sensitive information or performing unsafe actions.

## 1. Pop-Up Windows

Pop-Up Windows are messages or windows that appear on a user's screen to encourage them to click a link or perform a specific action.

Attackers may design these windows to look like legitimate system warnings or security notifications.

They may:

- Redirect users to fake websites.
- Request personal or login information.
- Encourage users to download malicious software.
- Ask users to sign in again or verify their identity.

After the user interacts with the malicious content, sensitive information may be collected and sent to an attacker-controlled website or email address.

## 2. Hoax Letters

A Hoax Letter is a misleading message that warns users about a fake threat, such as a non-existent computer virus.

Characteristics include:

- Spreading through social engineering.
- Creating unnecessary fear or confusion.
- Wasting users' and network administrators' time.
- Reducing organizational productivity.

The primary objective is often to create anxiety and encourage users to spread false information.

## 3. Spam Email

Spam Emails are unsolicited and unwanted messages sent to users.

Attackers may use spam emails to:

- Send malicious links designed to steal banking or login information.
- Attach files containing viruses, Trojans, or other malware.
- Disguise file extensions using misleading or excessively long filenames.

The objective is to trick users into opening malicious attachments, visiting harmful websites, or disclosing sensitive information.

## 4. Scareware

Scareware is malicious or deceptive software that uses fake warnings and frightening messages to manipulate users.

Common characteristics include:

- Displaying pop-ups claiming that the computer is infected.
- Pretending to represent legitimate security companies.
- Creating a sense of urgency.
- Encouraging users to download or execute malicious software.

The victim may install malware voluntarily because they believe they are protecting their system.

## 5. Instant Chat Messenger

Attackers may use online messaging platforms to communicate with selected victims and collect sensitive information.

They may attempt to obtain:

- Date of birth.
- Full name.
- Other personal information that could help compromise user accounts.

Collected information may later be used for account attacks or identity-related fraud.

## 6. Phishing and Related Techniques

The slides introduce the following techniques:

### Phishing

A social engineering technique in which attackers use deceptive messages or websites to trick users into revealing sensitive information.

### Spear Phishing

A targeted form of phishing directed at a specific individual or organization.

### Whaling

A targeted phishing technique that focuses on high-profile individuals, such as executives or senior managers.

### Pharming

A technique that redirects users to fraudulent websites, even when they attempt to visit legitimate websites.

### Spimming

Spam messages distributed through instant messaging platforms.

## Summary

Computer-Based Social Engineering uses digital communication and deceptive content to manipulate users.

Common methods include pop-up windows, hoax letters, spam emails, scareware, instant messaging attacks, and phishing-related techniques.

Users should verify unexpected messages, avoid suspicious links and attachments, and never provide sensitive information without confirming the legitimacy of the request.
