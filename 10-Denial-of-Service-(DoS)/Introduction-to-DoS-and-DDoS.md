
# Introduction to DoS and DDoS

## 1. What Is a Denial of Service (DoS) Attack?

A **Denial of Service (DoS)** attack is an attack against a computer or network that reduces, limits, or completely stops legitimate users from accessing system resources.

In this attack, the attacker floods the target system with illegitimate service requests or fake traffic. This overloads the system's resources and may cause it to become unavailable.

### Main Objective

The primary objective of a DoS attack is to disrupt the availability of a system, network, or service and prevent legitimate users from accessing it.

## 2. What Is a Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) Attack?

A **Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS)** attack is a coordinated, large-scale attack that disrupts access to services or network resources belonging to a target.

Unlike an attack originating from a single source, a DDoS attack uses a large number of compromised computers connected through the Internet. These compromised systems are commonly organized into a **botnet**.

### How Does It Work?

The course describes the following general process:

1. The attacker uses specialized scripts to identify vulnerable systems.
2. The attacker gains access to those systems.
3. DDoS software is installed on the compromised systems.
4. The compromised systems are coordinated to attack the target at an appropriate time.

This allows the attacker to generate traffic from multiple sources and overwhelm the target.

## Summary

- **DoS** attacks disrupt access to system or network resources by overwhelming them with illegitimate requests or traffic.
- **DDoS** attacks use multiple compromised systems to coordinate a large-scale disruption.
- Botnets can provide the distributed infrastructure used in DDoS attacks.
