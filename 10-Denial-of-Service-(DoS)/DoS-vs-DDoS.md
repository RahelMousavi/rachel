
# DoS vs. DDoS

Both **Denial of Service (DoS)** and **Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS)** attacks aim to disrupt the availability of a target system, network, or service.

The main difference is how the attack traffic is generated and where it originates.

## 1. DoS Attack

A DoS attack disrupts a target by flooding it with illegitimate requests or traffic.

In the basic DoS model, the attack originates from a single source.

## 2. DDoS Attack

A DDoS attack is coordinated and distributed across multiple sources.

It commonly uses compromised computers, also known as bots or zombies, which may be organized into a botnet.

## 3. Key Differences

- **Source of traffic:** A basic DoS attack originates from one source, while a DDoS attack uses multiple distributed sources.
- **Coordination:** A DoS attack does not require a botnet, whereas DDoS attacks commonly rely on coordinated compromised systems.
- **Scale:** DDoS attacks can generate traffic from many systems simultaneously, making the attack distributed across different sources.
- **Traffic identification:** Identifying and filtering malicious traffic can be more challenging when it originates from many different systems.

## Summary

The key distinction between DoS and DDoS is the distribution of attack sources.

- **DoS:** An attack originating from a single source in the basic model.
- **DDoS:** A coordinated attack originating from multiple distributed sources.

Both attacks target the availability of systems and services.
