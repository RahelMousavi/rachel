# Ethical Hacking

## 1. What Is an Ethical Hacker?

An **Ethical Hacker** is a cybersecurity professional who uses hacking techniques and tools to identify security vulnerabilities in computer systems, networks, and applications with the permission of their owners.

The primary goal of ethical hacking is to discover security weaknesses before malicious attackers can exploit them.

Ethical hackers simulate real-world attacks in a controlled and authorized manner to evaluate the security of an organization's systems.

## 2. Differences Between Ethical Hackers and Other Hackers

The main difference between ethical hackers and other hackers is their **authorization, intention, and objectives**.

- **White Hat Hackers:** Work with proper authorization to identify vulnerabilities and improve system security.
- **Gray Hat Hackers:** May access systems without permission to discover vulnerabilities, but do not necessarily have malicious intentions.
- **Black Hat Hackers:** Gain unauthorized access to systems for malicious purposes, such as stealing information, damaging systems, or financial gain.

An ethical hacker must always have permission from the system owner, follow the agreed scope of testing, protect sensitive information, and report all findings to the client.

## 3. Steps of Ethical Hacking

An ethical hacking engagement typically follows these steps:

### 3.1 Client Consultation and Requirement Gathering

The ethical hacker communicates with the client to understand their security needs, objectives, and expectations.

During this stage, the following details are discussed:

- Target systems and networks.
- Scope and limitations of the assessment.
- Testing objectives.
- Available resources and required timeline.
- Potential risks and restrictions.

### 3.2 Signing a Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA)

A **Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA)** is a legal agreement between the ethical hacker and the client.

It ensures that confidential information obtained during the engagement is not disclosed to unauthorized parties.

### 3.3 Information Gathering

The ethical hacker collects information about the target organization, systems, networks, and applications.

This information helps identify potential attack surfaces and prepare for the penetration testing phase.

### 3.4 Penetration Testing

The ethical hacker performs authorized security tests to identify and validate vulnerabilities in the target environment.

Various tools and techniques may be used to simulate real-world attacks within the agreed scope.

### 3.5 Analysis and Evaluation

The discovered vulnerabilities and test results are analyzed to determine their severity, potential impact, and associated risks.

Findings are prioritized based on their level of risk and the potential consequences of exploitation.

### 3.6 Report Preparation and Presentation

The ethical hacker prepares a detailed report and presents the results to the client.

The report typically includes:

- Identified vulnerabilities.
- Evidence and technical details.
- Risk levels and potential impacts.
- Recommended solutions and mitigation strategies.
- Summary of the overall security assessment.

The purpose of the final report is to help the client understand security weaknesses and take appropriate corrective actions.

## 4. Summary

Ethical hacking is an authorized security assessment performed to identify and reduce security risks before malicious attackers can exploit them.

Unlike malicious hackers, ethical hackers work with permission, follow an agreed scope, protect confidential information, and report their findings to the system owners.

The six main stages of an ethical hacking engagement are:

1. Client Consultation and Requirement Gathering
2. Signing an NDA
3. Information Gathering
4. Penetration Testing
5. Analysis and Evaluation
6. Report Preparation and Presentation
