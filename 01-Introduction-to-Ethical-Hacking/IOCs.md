# Indicators of Compromise (IOCs)

## 1. Overview

An **Indicator of Compromise (IOC)** is a piece of evidence or a sign that suggests a system, network, or device may have been compromised or involved in malicious activity.
IOCs help security professionals identify potential security breaches, investigate cyberattacks, and detect malicious activities within an organization's environment.
They can be identified by analyzing network traffic, system logs, email messages, and files.

## 2. Types of Indicators of Compromise

### 2.1 Network Indicators

**Network Indicators** are signs found in network traffic and communications that may indicate connections to malicious infrastructure or suspicious network activities.

**Examples:**

- Suspicious IP Addresses
- Malicious Domains
- Malicious URLs
- Unusual DNS Requests
- Connections to Command and Control (C2) Servers

### 2.2 Email Indicators

**Email Indicators** are suspicious characteristics found in email messages that may indicate phishing attempts or other email-based attacks.

**Examples:**

- Suspicious Sender Addresses
- Malicious Email Subjects
- Suspicious Email Links
- Malicious Attachments
- Spoofed Email Addresses

### 2.3 Host-Based Indicators

**Host-Based Indicators** are signs found directly on computers, servers, or other devices that may indicate malware infections or unauthorized access.

**Examples:**

- Malicious File Hashes
- Suspicious Registry Keys
- Unusual Processes
- Malicious DLL Files
- Unauthorized File Modifications

### 2.4 Behavioral Indicators

**Behavioral Indicators** are unusual activities or patterns of behavior performed by users, applications, or systems that may indicate malicious activity.

**Examples:**

- Unusual PowerShell Commands
- Unexpected Script Execution
- Unusual Login Activities
- Unauthorized Privilege Escalation
- Abnormal Data Transfers

## 3. Summary

Indicators of Compromise help security professionals identify potential security breaches and investigate malicious activities.
The four main categories of IOCs are:

- **Network Indicators:** Suspicious IP addresses, domains, URLs, and network connections.
- **Email Indicators:** Suspicious senders, email links, subjects, and attachments.
- **Host-Based Indicators:** Malicious files, registry changes, processes, and DLL files.
- **Behavioral Indicators:** Unusual commands, scripts, login activities, and system behavior.

Monitoring and analyzing IOCs helps organizations detect potential compromises and respond to security incidents more effectively.
