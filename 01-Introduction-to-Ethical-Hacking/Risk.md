# Risk and Risk Management

## 1. What Is Risk?

**Risk** is the possibility that an uncertain event may occur and cause damage to a system, organization, or its assets.
Risk assessment considers both the probability of an event occurring and the severity of its consequences.
Risk helps organizations identify potential threats, prioritize security measures, and use their resources effectively.
Two main factors are used to evaluate risk:

- **Probability:** The likelihood that a threat or unwanted event will occur, usually expressed as a percentage.
- **Consequences:** The severity of the damage or impact that may result from the event.

## 2. Risk Levels

Risk is generally classified into four levels:

### 2.1 Low

A low level of risk requires preventive measures to minimize its potential effects.

### 2.2 Medium

A medium level of risk does not require immediate action, but appropriate security controls should be implemented as soon as possible.

### 2.3 High

A high level of risk requires immediate attention and security measures to reduce the risk to an acceptable level.

### 2.4 Extreme

An extreme level of risk requires immediate action and strong security controls to reduce its potential impact.

## 3. Risk Matrix

A **Risk Matrix** is a tool used to determine the level of risk by combining the probability of an event with the severity of its consequences.

### 3.1 Probability Levels

Probability represents the likelihood of an event occurring.

| Probability Range | Probability Level |
|---|---|
| 81% - 100% | Very High Probability |
| 61% - 80% | High Probability |
| 41% - 60% | Moderate Probability |
| 21% - 40% | Low Probability |
| 1% - 20% | Very Low Probability |

### 3.2 Consequence Levels

Consequences represent the severity of the potential damage.

The matrix uses five levels:

- **Insignificant:** Very little or negligible impact.
- **Minor:** Small impact with limited damage.
- **Moderate:** Noticeable impact requiring attention.
- **Major:** Serious impact causing significant damage.
- **Severe:** Extremely serious impact with potentially critical consequences.

### 3.3 Risk Matrix Table

| Probability | Insignificant | Minor | Moderate | Major | Severe |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 81% - 100% | Low | Medium | High | Extreme | Extreme |
| 61% - 80% | Low | Medium | High | High | Extreme |
| 41% - 60% | Low | Medium | Medium | High | High |
| 21% - 40% | Low | Low | Medium | Medium | High |
| 1% - 20% | Low | Low | Medium | Medium | High |

### 3.4 Actions Based on Risk Levels

- **Extreme or High:** Immediate measures should be taken to address the risk. Appropriate security controls must be identified and implemented to reduce the risk to a reasonably low level.
- **Medium:** No urgent action is required, but security controls should be implemented as soon as possible to reduce the risk.
- **Low:** Preventive measures should be taken to minimize the potential effects of the risk.

## 4. Risk Management

**Risk Management** is the process of identifying, assessing, treating, tracking, and reviewing risks to reduce their potential impact on an organization.
It consists of five main stages:

### 4.1 Identification

**Risk Identification** involves identifying potential risks, threats, vulnerabilities, and assets that may be affected.

### 4.2 Assessment

**Risk Assessment** involves evaluating identified risks by analyzing their probability and potential consequences.
The purpose is to determine the risk level and prioritize risks that require attention.

### 4.3 Treatment

**Risk Treatment** involves selecting and implementing appropriate strategies to manage identified risks.
Common risk treatment strategies include:

- **Risk Avoidance:** Eliminating an activity that introduces a particular risk.
- **Risk Mitigation:** Reducing the probability or impact of a risk.
- **Risk Transfer:** Transferring some of the financial or operational consequences of a risk to another party, such as through insurance.
- **Risk Acceptance:** Accepting a risk when its potential impact is considered manageable or the cost of reducing it is too high.

### 4.4 Tracking

**Risk Tracking** involves continuously monitoring identified risks and checking the effectiveness of implemented security controls.

### 4.5 Review

**Risk Review** involves periodically reassessing risks and reviewing security measures to identify changes, new threats, and emerging vulnerabilities.

## 5. Summary

Risk management is a continuous process that helps organizations identify potential threats, evaluate their impact, and implement appropriate security measures.
The five main stages of Risk Management are:

1. **Identification**
2. **Assessment**
3. **Treatment**
4. **Tracking**
5. **Review**

A Risk Matrix helps determine risk levels by combining Probability and Consequences.
