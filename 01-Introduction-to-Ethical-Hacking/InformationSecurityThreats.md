
# Information Security Threats

## 1. Overview

**Information Security Threats** are potential events or actions that can compromise the confidentiality, integrity, or availability (CIA) of information and information systems.
Information security threats can originate from natural events, physical incidents, human activities, or attacks targeting networks, hosts, and applications.

## 2. Types of Information Security Threats

### 2.1 Natural Threats

**Natural Threats** are threats caused by natural events that may damage equipment, information, and organizational infrastructure.

**Examples:**
- **Natural Disasters:** Natural events that can damage infrastructure.
- **Floods:** Water damage to equipment and data centers.
- **Earthquakes:** Physical damage to buildings and IT infrastructure.
- **Hurricanes:** Severe storms that can disrupt systems and services.

**Example:** A flood damages a data center and destroys servers and stored information.

### 2.2 Physical Security Threats

These threats involve physical access to, damage to, or loss of hardware, facilities, and other physical resources.

**Examples:**
- **Loss or Damage of System Resources:** Loss, theft, or damage of hardware and system resources.
- **Physical Intrusion:** Unauthorized physical access to restricted areas.
- **Sabotage:** Intentional damage to equipment or infrastructure.
- **Espionage:** Secretly collecting sensitive information through unauthorized access.
- **Errors:** Human mistakes that cause physical damage or system disruption.

**Example:** An unauthorized person enters a server room and steals hard drives.

### 2.3 Human Threats

**Human Threats** result from intentional or accidental human activities that may compromise information security.

**Examples:**
- **Hackers:** Individuals who use various techniques and tools to attack computer systems.
- **Insiders:** Employees or authorized individuals who may intentionally or accidentally compromise security.
- **Social Engineering:** Manipulating people into revealing confidential information or performing unsafe actions.
- **Lack of Knowledge and Awareness:** Insufficient security knowledge that may lead to unsafe behavior.

**Example:** An employee clicks a malicious link in a phishing email because of a lack of security awareness.

### 2.4 Network Threats

**Network Threats** are attacks that target network infrastructure, communications, and data transmission.

**Examples:**
- **Information Gathering:** Collecting information about networks, systems, and services to identify potential targets.
- **Sniffing:** Capturing and analyzing network traffic to obtain transmitted information.
- **Spoofing:** Impersonating another system or user to deceive other systems.
- **Session Hijacking:** Taking control of a user's active session to gain unauthorized access.

**Example:** An attacker uses sniffing to collect sensitive information transmitted over an insecure network.

### 2.5 Host Threats

**Host Threats** target computer systems, servers, and operating systems.

**Examples:**
- **Malware Attacks:** Attacks involving malicious software such as viruses, Trojans, and ransomware.
- **Password Attacks:** Attempts to discover or steal user passwords.
- **DoS Attacks:** Disrupting system availability by exhausting its resources.
- **Backdoor Attacks:** Creating or exploiting hidden access mechanisms to gain unauthorized access to a system.

**Example:** A Trojan is installed on a server to provide an attacker with unauthorized access.

### 2.6 Application Threats

**Application Threats** involve vulnerabilities and attacks targeting software and applications.

**Examples:**
- **Buffer Overflow:** Occurs when more data is written to a memory buffer than it can hold, potentially causing system crashes or malicious code execution.
- **Input Validation:** Improper input validation can allow attacks such as SQL Injection and Cross-Site Scripting (XSS).
- **Authentication:** Weak authentication mechanisms may allow attackers to impersonate users or gain unauthorized access.
- **Authorization:** Improper access control may allow users to access resources they are not authorized to use.

**Example:** An attacker exploits improper input validation to perform SQL Injection against a web application.

## 3. Summary

Information Security Threats can be classified into six main categories:

- **Natural Threats:** Threats caused by natural events.
- **Physical Security Threats:** Threats involving physical resources and facilities.
- **Human Threats:** Threats resulting from human activities and mistakes.
- **Network Threats:** Attacks targeting networks and communications.
- **Host Threats:** Attacks targeting computers, servers, and operating systems.
- **Application Threats:** Attacks targeting software and applications.

Understanding these threats helps organizations identify potential risks and implement appropriate security controls to protect their information and systems.
