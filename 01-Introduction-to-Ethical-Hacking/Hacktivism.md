# Hacktivism and Hacker Categories

## 1. Hacktivism

**Hacktivism** is the combination of the words *Hacking* and *Activism*.

It refers to the use of hacking techniques and digital technologies to promote a political, social, religious, or ideological cause.

Hacktivists usually aim to raise awareness, express opposition, expose information, or influence public opinion through cyber activities.

**Common Hacktivist Activities:**

- Website Defacement
- Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS)
- Data Leaks
- Unauthorized Access
- Digital Protests

## 2. Hacker Categories

Hackers are commonly classified into three main categories based on their intentions and the legality of their activities.

### 2.1 White Hat Hackers

**White Hat Hackers** are ethical hackers who use their technical skills to identify security vulnerabilities and improve system security.

They work with proper authorization and follow legal and ethical guidelines.

**Common Characteristics:**
- Work with permission from system owners.
- Identify and report security vulnerabilities.
- Help organizations improve their security.

### 2.2 Gray Hat Hackers

**Gray Hat Hackers** operate between ethical and unethical hacking.

They may identify or access vulnerabilities without authorization, but they do not necessarily intend to cause harm or steal information.

Even if their intentions are not malicious, accessing systems without permission may still be illegal.

**Common Characteristics:**
- May access systems without authorization.
- Usually do not intend to cause serious damage.
- May report discovered vulnerabilities to system owners.

### 2.3 Black Hat Hackers

**Black Hat Hackers** use their technical skills for malicious or illegal purposes.

Their objectives may include stealing information, financial gain, damaging systems, or disrupting services.

**Common Characteristics:**
- Gain unauthorized access to systems.
- Steal, modify, or destroy information.
- Develop or distribute malicious software.
- Exploit vulnerabilities for personal or malicious purposes.

## 3. Other Hacker-Related Terms

The following terms describe different hacker roles or behaviors. They are not direct equivalents of White Hat, Gray Hat, or Black Hat hackers.

- **Spy Hackers:** Hackers who conduct cyber espionage to secretly collect sensitive information, often for governments, organizations, or other entities.
- **Cyber Terrorists:** Individuals or groups that use cyberattacks to create fear, disruption, or serious damage in pursuit of ideological or political objectives.
- **Script Kiddies:** Inexperienced individuals who use existing hacking tools, scripts, or exploits developed by others, often without fully understanding how they work.

## 4. Summary

Hacktivism uses hacking techniques to support political, social, or ideological causes.

Hackers can be classified into three main categories:

- **White Hat:** Ethical hackers who work with authorization.
- **Gray Hat:** Hackers who may access systems without permission but do not necessarily have malicious intentions.
- **Black Hat:** Hackers who perform malicious or illegal activities.

Other terms such as Spy Hackers, Cyber Terrorists, and Script Kiddies describe specific motivations, objectives, or skill levels rather than the three main hacker categories.
