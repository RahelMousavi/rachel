
# Wi-Fi Standards and Frequency

## Introduction to Wi-Fi and IEEE 802.11

Wi-Fi refers to wireless networks commonly used indoors. These networks are based on the IEEE 802.11 standards.

Devices such as PCs, game consoles, and mobile phones use Wi-Fi to access network resources.

## Service Set Identifier (SSID)

SSID is the name used to identify a Wi-Fi network.

- It is included in the wireless frame header by default.
- It is a shared identifier between a wireless client and an Access Point (AP).
- Access Points continuously broadcast their SSID so that clients can discover and connect to the network.
- An SSID can contain a maximum of 32 bytes.
- If the SSID is changed, connected computers must also update their network configuration.

By default, an SSID often contains the manufacturer's name. Leaving the default SSID unchanged may create security risks.

## Radio Frequency (RF)

Frequency is the number of times an event repeats within a specific unit of time.

The unit of frequency is **Hertz (Hz)**.

- One Hertz represents one repetition per second.
- The Radio Frequency (RF) range is between 20 kHz and 300 GHz.
- Radio waves can travel through a conductor.
- Antennas transmit radio signals into the air.

An antenna converts wired signals into wireless signals and wireless signals back into wired signals.

RF waves radiate from an antenna in straight lines and in all directions simultaneously.

## Wireless Frequency Bands

The module introduces the following wireless frequency bands:

### 2.4 GHz

The 2.4 GHz frequency band is associated with these IEEE 802.11 standards:

- 802.11b
- 802.11g
- 802.11n

### 5 GHz

The 5 GHz frequency band is associated with these IEEE 802.11 standards:

- 802.11a
- 802.11h
- 802.11j
- 802.11n

## Summary

Wi-Fi networks are based on IEEE 802.11 standards.

SSID identifies a wireless network and is commonly broadcast by Access Points. Wireless communication uses radio frequencies, and antennas convert signals between wired and wireless forms.

The module identifies two main frequency bands: 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz.
