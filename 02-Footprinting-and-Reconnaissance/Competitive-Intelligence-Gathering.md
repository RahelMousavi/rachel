
# Competitive Intelligence Gathering

Competitive Intelligence Gathering is the process of identifying, collecting, analyzing, and verifying information about competitors using publicly available and legal sources.

It helps organizations understand their competitors, business activities, products, strategies, and market position.

## Sources of Competitive Intelligence

The following sources can be used to gather competitive information:

- **Company Websites and Job Advertisements:** Provide information about company activities, services, technologies, and recruitment needs.
- **Search Engines and Online Databases:** Help discover publicly available information about competitors.
- **News Reports and Annual Reports:** Provide details about company performance, business activities, and important developments.
- **Conferences, Exhibitions, and Newspapers:** Offer information about industry trends, products, and business strategies.
- **Patents and Trademarks:** Reveal information about innovations, technologies, and registered products.
- **Product Catalogs and Stores:** Provide information about competitors' products, features, and pricing.
- **Analytical and Statistical Reports:** Help understand market trends and competitor performance.
- **Customer and Vendor Interviews:** Can provide insights into customer experiences and business relationships.
- **Representatives, Distributors, and Suppliers:** May provide information about products, services, and supply chains.

## Role in Footprinting

Competitive Intelligence Gathering can help security researchers:

- Understand an organization's business activities.
- Identify technologies and products used by an organization.
- Discover publicly available organizational information.
- Identify business relationships and external partners.
- Understand the organization's market presence.

## Summary

Competitive Intelligence Gathering involves collecting and analyzing information about competitors from public and legal sources.

During Footprinting, it helps researchers understand an organization's activities, products, technologies, business relationships, and external environment.
